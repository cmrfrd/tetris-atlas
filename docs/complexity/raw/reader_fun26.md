## model

THE PAPER USES TWO MOVEMENT MODELS, BOTH FAR MORE PERMISSIVE THAN SKYLINE HARD-DROP.

(1) Super Rotation System (SRS), used for ALL tetromino hardness results (Sections 5-9). Base game model (p.1): a piece spawns at the top of the grid in a default orientation and "periodically moves down one unit"; between gravity ticks the player may repeatedly move the piece one unit left, one unit right, one unit down, or rotate +/-90 degrees. A piece locks ONLY when it attempts to move downward while resting on a filled square — so unlimited horizontal slides, tucks under overhangs, and rotations at any depth are allowed before lock. Loss = a piece locks above a certain height or where the next piece would spawn (checked at lock, NOT after clears — stricter than the tetris-atlas rule). Rotation is SRS with kicks (Section 2.1, p.3): each rotation attempt sequentially tests 5 offset positions (Table 1 for J/L/S/T/Z, Table 2 for I; Figure 44 visualizes all 8 rotations x 5 tests for I); if all fail the rotation fails. Kicks enable "twists"/"spins", including: I pieces rotating about either END square (tests 4/5), letting an I "turn corners" inside 1-wide tunnels (p.15, Appendix B.1); J/L/T pieces "climbing staircases" (p.2: results "rely crucially on the rotation system, in particular, the ability with SRS to climb up certain 'staircases' using 'kicks'"; Appendices C, D); S/Z spin-tucks (Appendix E). No hold and no preview in the base model, but the reductions are robust to hold and even to arbitrary permutation of the sequence (p.2, p.24). Perfect information: the whole finite sequence is given (offline).

(2) Falling rotation model for dominoes only (Section 2.2, p.4): a rotating domino keeps one lowest pixel fixed and the other pixel moves to a neighboring not-higher cell — pieces get monotonically lower when rotating. Used only for the positive (poly-time) domino results; dominoes can traverse any monotonically-descending path of empty cells (Lemma 3.2, p.7), i.e., full mid-fall lateral movement, way beyond skyline.

Objectives (p.2): "Tetris clearing" = can the player clear the ENTIRE board after placing all given pieces; "Tetris survival" = can the player avoid losing while placing all given pieces. Both take an INITIAL PARTIALLY-FILLED BOARD and an explicit finite sequence as input. Piece budgets are exact (M/4 pieces for M empty squares), so clearing = perfect tiling that is also maneuverable under Tetris rules.

## theorems

MAIN THEOREMS (all offline, given initial board + explicit sequence):

- Theorem 3.1 (p.5): POSITIVE. If board height >= 2k-1 and the top k-1 rows are initially empty, the player can survive an INFINITE sequence of 1xk pieces, and there is a poly-time algorithm to decide eventual full clearing. Strategy: phase 1 eliminates holes top-down using only vertical 1xk drops into columns; phase 2 greedily places horizontal pieces column-by-column to equalize filled-cell counts mod k (k guesses for the first column). Corollary: the I NP-hardness result NEEDS filled cells in the top 3 rows.
- Theorem 3.6 / Corollary 3.7 (p.9): POSITIVE. Poly-time algorithms for Tetris survival and eventual clearability with rotatable dominoes (falling rotation model), and survival with any finite number k of dominoes. Uses path-order/supported-configuration machinery (Lemmas 3.2-3.5) + O(n^3) DP over horizontal-domino "fixes".
- Theorem 4.5 (p.10): Planar Biconnected {{0,4}-in-4, 1-in-4} and {{0,4}-in-4, 3-in-4} Graph Orientation are NP-hard (source problems; also 1-in-3SAT via [HIN+17] Graph Orientation, and Planar Monotone Rectilinear 3SAT).
- Theorem 5.1 (p.12): Tetris clearing with SRS is NP-hard even if the sequence is restricted to just I pieces. Reduction from 1-in-3SAT via Graph Orientation on a grid; board is 60R+10 rows x 48C+16 columns; disproves the 23-year-old conjecture from [BDH+04] that I-only Tetris is poly-time (for this rotation system).
- Theorem 5.2 (p.22, Section 5.5 "Aside: Bags"): Tetris clearing with SRS is NP-hard even if the piece sequence must be able to be generated from the 7-bag randomizer. Sequence = N+5 bags = 7N+35 pieces, where N+1 = number of I pieces needed to clear the Theorem 5.1 structure. Order of pieces WITHIN each bag does not matter; robust to hold and to full permutation. Remark (p.24): extends to 7k-bag randomizers for any k >= 1 (k parallel copies of the absorber, only one leading to the core).
- Theorem 6.1 (p.25): clearing NP-hard with just J (or just L, by symmetry), from Planar Biconnected {{0,4}-in-4, 3-in-4} Graph Orientation.
- Theorem 7.1 (p.30): clearing NP-hard with just T, from Planar Biconnected {{0,4}-in-4, 1-in-4} Graph Orientation.
- Theorem 8.1 (p.35): clearing NP-hard with just S (or just Z), from Planar Monotone Rectilinear 3SAT.
- Theorem 9.1 (p.39): Tetris SURVIVAL with SRS is NP-hard for any single piece type P in {I, J, L, S, T, Z}. For I/J/L/T: same construction with the rightmost two columns turned into a checkerboard (prevents placements there and prevents ALL line clears), one fewer piece. For S/Z: remove the climbable vertical structure; attach a (2C1+2)x(2C2+2) empty rectangle to each of the m clause gadgets and give mC1C2 pieces; fitting them all without losing forces every clause gadget to be accessible.
- O piece: explicitly EXCLUDED everywhere ("for any tetromino piece type P except O"); complexity open (Section 10).
- Open problems (Section 10, pp.40-41): (a) rotatable dominoes under other rotation models (possibly NP-hard); (b) O-piece clearing (plausibly P, row-clearing mechanic is the obstacle) and O survival (evidence of NP-hardness via 2x2 square packing in orthogonally convex grid polygons [AS26]); (c) #P-hardness / ASP-completeness (construction is not c-monious); (d) infinite piece sequences of a given type — complexity open. NOTABLY ABSENT: nothing on survival under bag randomizers, other randomizers, online/adversarial play, or empty-board dimension-only instances. The only online reference is [GV24] (no online algorithm can be competitive) and the harddrop.com "Playing forever" wiki [DTW].

## gadgets

I-TRIS CORE (Theorem 5.1, the base of the bag result). Global layout (Fig 10, p.13): the "main part" (green) encodes the Graph Orientation instance; a "long tunnel" — a snaking 1-wide path of empty squares through the leftmost 12 columns / topmost 8 rows — is the ONLY access from spawn to the main part; its entrance width admits only I pieces. 4 extra empty squares in the second-to-rightmost column must be filled last, after middle rows clear. Piece budget exact (M/4). Key facts: the long tunnel has a UNIQUE I-tiling (Fig 11); any I locked in the tunnel early blocks the board forever; the tunnel prevents premature line clears. THE CENTRAL TRAJECTORY MECHANIC (p.15): I pieces "turn corners" — SRS kick tests 4/5 act as rotations about an END square of the I, so an I can snake through 1-wide bent tunnels via repeated end-pivot rotations (Maneuver 1, Fig 45, 8 stages, reversible). This is a KICK TRAVERSAL UNDER FULL OVERHANG — the piece moves horizontally and around corners deep inside covered corridors. Gadgets (Section 5.2, Figs 12-20; tilings Figs 61-68): corner (LC/UC), entry corner (EC), vertical line (VL), horizontal line (HL), duplicator, clause, negated-clause, crossover — modeled on the 2BAR-tiling gadgets of [HIN+17], each with dotted "ports" and exactly 2 tilings (bit = which port is covered); wires of length 3 mod 4 transmit the bit between gadgets. Every gadget requires I pieces to ENTER through a port and maneuver INSIDE covered corridors: duplicator needs specific piece orientations at slots 48/60, fixable by two counterclockwise end-pivot rotations (Maneuver 2, Fig 46); negated-clause piece 15 needs an orientation-sensitive traversal (Maneuver 3, Fig 47); crossover has three 9x5 "chambers" and needs pre-placed I pieces (left or right version, Fig 20) so that all wall-kick tests before the desired one FAIL (deliberate kick-table engineering), plus Maneuver 4 (Fig 48) to thread chambers. Forcing = exact budget + unique tilings + blocked-tunnel death + fill order (lower part per Fig 21, then upper part) that keeps every gadget reachable.

BAG ABSORBER (Theorem 5.2, Figs 22-24, pp.22-24). Modify the top two rows of the Thm 5.1 structure and add 2N+4 lines on top; the top 2N lines are ~the same 2-line stratum repeated N times. Each stratum contains six dedicated slots — one per non-I tetromino — sitting at the mouths of six vertical tunnels that run down toward the entrance of the main reduction structure. Per bag (any internal order): the six non-I pieces MUST land in their designated slots in the topmost two rows. Forcing chain: the O placement is forced (any other O placement sticks out over the topmost line, unusable cells => clearing impossible); the other five non-I pieces must be placed so the topmost two rows CLEAR so the NEXT bag's O can be placed (Fig 23 shows the forced placements). The I piece routing: if the I comes first or last in the bag, all six tunnels are open; otherwise at most 5 non-I pieces have been placed, so at least one of the six tunnels is still open — the I descends through an open tunnel and is then maneuvered "into the entrance of the main reduction structure", from where it snakes through the long tunnel via the kick maneuvers of Appendix B.1. Invariant: after every bag, the top 2 lines have cleared and EXACTLY ONE additional I has been delivered to the core. Only I pieces fit through the long-tunnel entrance, so non-I pieces can never pollute the core. First N bags feed the core (N+1 I's needed: the +1 comes from the pre-modification), last 5 bags are pure cleanup of the remaining absorber lines (Fig 24). Trajectory demands: non-I slot placements are near-top drops into open notches (mild — mostly straight-drop-like, some slides); the I routing is the hard part — vertical shaft descent, then horizontal covered-corridor traversal + corner turning by SRS end-pivot kicks.

J/L-TRIS (Sec 6): long tunnel + "climbable vertical structure" (unique J tiling, Figs 26/27); Maneuver C.1 (Fig 49, 11 stages) CLIMBS J pieces UP the staircase segment-by-segment using rotations + wall kicks; corner-turn (Fig 50) and tuck (Fig 51) maneuvers; gadgets = HL, U-turns, EC, 0-or-4, 3-in-4, two parity fixers; graph embedded with the BICONN algorithm giving a "downward reachable" drawing, filled in reverse BFS order. T-TRIS (Sec 7): same skeleton with T gadgets (0-or-4, 1-in-4, HL, U-turn, EC, 2 parity fixers, climbable vertical structure Figs 33/34); Maneuvers D (Figs 52-54) push T pieces UPWARD or tuck them via kicks. S/Z-TRIS (Sec 8): variable gadget = two-tunnel structure whose True/False state is set by where one initial S is placed (Fig 40); clause gadget with 3 entrances; diagonal-staircase climbable vertical structure gates all line clears; Maneuvers E.1-E.6 move S pieces upward/sideways through gaps by spin-tucks. SURVIVAL variants (Sec 9): checkerboarded right columns kill all line clears (I/J/L/T); S/Z uses clause-attached empty rectangles + counting.

## compat

ASSESSMENT FOR THE TETRIS-ATLAS SKYLINE HARD-DROP NO-KICK MODEL (rotation chosen pre-drop; straight vertical descent onto column-height skyline; never enters cavities under overhangs; no slides/spins/soft-drop; lock on contact; loss checked AFTER clears):

BREAKS — essentially every hardness gadget in this paper:
1. Theorem 5.1 (I-only core): fatally incompatible. The entire construction lives under overhangs: the long tunnel, all eight gadget types, and the wires are covered 1-wide corridors reachable only by SRS end-pivot kick traversal (Maneuver 1) and horizontal slides. In the skyline model a piece can NEVER be under a filled cell of another column's overhang at lock unless it created the hole itself from above; the main part of the construction is simply unreachable. The paper itself flags this (p.2): results "rely crucially on the rotation system, in particular, the ability with SRS to climb up certain 'staircases' using 'kicks'". Also, the pre-filled board it needs has cells in the top three rows (their own Theorem 3.1 shows this is necessary), so even the board shape is adversarially engineered.
2. Theorem 5.2 (7-bag): breaks with the core, and its own routing breaks independently: (a) the I piece must traverse covered tunnels/corridors horizontally after descending the shaft — impossible skyline; (b) the O-forcing argument uses their loss/clearing semantics where cells locked above the topmost line are dead — in our engine a transient poke above the ceiling that clears back is legal, so the forcing needs re-derivation (for the CLEARING objective the budget argument still mostly works: wasted cells above the structure can never be cleared, so O-forcing survives for clearing, but NOT for survival variants); (c) delivery into the core needs the kick corner-turn. WHAT SURVIVES AS A BLUEPRINT: the bag-absorber IDEA is largely skyline-compatible in isolation — six dedicated open-from-above slots per 2-row stratum, each slot a straight drop for one non-I piece; per-bag forced clearing of the top stratum; six redundant vertical shafts guaranteeing >=1 open route for the useful piece regardless of within-bag order; exactly one useful piece per bag delivered downward; k-fold parallelization for 7k-bags. If one had a skyline-hard computation core fed by straight vertical drops down an open shaft, this absorber ports over with only the O-forcing re-analysis. This is the single most reusable artifact of the paper for P1'.
3. Theorems 6.1/7.1/8.1 (J/L, T, S/Z): all require climbing structures (pieces move UPWARD via kicks) and spin-tucks — maximally incompatible with skyline.
4. Theorem 9.1 (survival): built on the same cores — incompatible. Additionally its checkerboard no-clear trick conflicts with nothing in our model per se (a checkerboard column pattern that blocks clears is model-independent board geometry), but the piece maneuvering into the rest of the structure is kick-dependent.

SURVIVES / PORTS:
5. Theorem 3.1 (1xk survival + clearing with top k-1 rows empty): the strategy is skyline-compatible as far as I can verify: phase 1 drops only VERTICAL 1xk pieces into columns whose cells above the target are all empty (straight drops); phase 2 drops horizontal pieces which rest on the max of their footprint columns (exactly skyline semantics; holes created are then re-eliminated by phase 1). Rotation is chosen per-piece before the drop. So the qualitative message ports: with only I pieces and a near-empty top, survival is easy and clearing decidable in P — consistent with I being the "drain" piece in the Atlas program. (Caveat: their board has no bag; and their loss rule is stricter than ours, which only helps survival in our model.)
6. The SOURCE problems (1-in-3SAT, the two Planar Biconnected Graph Orientation variants — new NP-hardness in Theorem 4.5 — and Planar Monotone Rectilinear 3SAT) are model-independent and reusable for any future skyline-hardness attempt.
7. Domino results: falling-rotation model, not skyline, and dominoes are outside our piece set — irrelevant but NOT portable as-is (Lemma 3.2 path traversal is full lateral movement).

CONSEQUENCES FOR THE PROBLEM FAMILY:
- P1 OFFLINE-HD: NOT resolved by this paper — its hardness is SRS-only. But note their related-work line (p.2): [MDHL24] "Tetris with few piece types" (FUN 2024) proves NP-hardness with any TWO piece types "also when restricted to dropping pieces or very high 'gravity'" — that is the citation to chase for hard-drop offline hardness (2 piece types, not 1, and sequences not bag-legal). One-piece-type offline in the skyline model remains open in both directions; this paper's positive Theorem 3.1 even suggests I-only skyline instances with empty top rows are in P.
- P1' OFFLINE-HD-BAG: open. Theorem 5.2 does not transfer, but its absorber blueprint + a skyline-hard core (e.g., adapting MDHL24's drop-model gadgets to be fed one useful piece per bag) is the natural attack; the within-bag order-freeness ("The order of pieces within each bag does not matter here") is exactly the robustness needed against the bag adversary's ordering freedom.
- P2 ONLINE-HORIZON / P3 ATLAS-MEMBERSHIP: untouched. The paper is purely offline/perfect-information; its only online citation is [GV24] (non-competitiveness). The infinite-sequence question they list as open (Section 10) is the single-type offline analogue of P3, still open even under SRS.
- P4 EMPTY-DIMS: untouched; all constructions need large adversarially pre-filled boards (with filled cells near the top), which are plausibly not even reachable states in the skyline model.
