## model

The paper defines FOUR movement regimes, all in the perfect-information OFFLINE setting (input = initial board of filled cells + explicit full piece sequence; introduced in [BDH+04]):

(1) STANDARD/SRS model (Sections 2, 4, 6 — the default for every "Tetris clearing with SRS" result): A tetromino spawns at the top and periodically moves down one unit. The player may repeatedly move the piece one unit left/right/down or rotate ±90 degrees, i.e., unlimited mid-fall maneuvering (tucks, slides, spins). Locking: when any part of the piece rests on top of a filled square long enough to trigger an automatic downward move, it locks (this "infinite-spin-until-lock" slack is what enables multi-step spin sequences). Loss: piece stops above a certain height or where the next piece would spawn. Full rows clear and rows above shift down. Rotation system = Super Rotation System (SRS, Tetris Guideline): each piece has a rotation center (I and O rotate about the center of their 4x4 box; O cannot rotate at all); if a rotation is obstructed, the game tries 4 alternate kick positions sequentially (Tables 2-3 give the full kick data, including VERTICAL offsets up to +-2), else the rotation fails. Kicks only fire when test 1 = (0,0) is obstructed by terrain — in an open spawn area rotations are pure. Kicks enable "twists/spins" (Appendix A) that move pieces sideways/downward into cavities under overhangs. No hold, no soft-drop distinction (down-move is just a move), no randomizer — the sequence is given.

(2) "HARD DROPS ONLY" mode (defined p.2): pieces do NOT move downward on their own; the piece sits at the spawn row where the player may freely rotate and translate left/right; the FIRST downward input is a hard drop — the piece moves maximally downward and LOCKS IMMEDIATELY. So: no soft drop, no mid-fall movement, no tucks, no last-second rotations. Kicks technically still exist (it's still SRS) but can only fire if the spawn-row rotation is obstructed by terrain reaching the top; in every construction the paper uses in this mode, the top rows are empty and the authors explicitly state the setups "do not require the kicking system of SRS." Hence in this mode a placement = (piece, rotation, column) followed by a straight cell-collision drop from above the stack — which is mathematically identical to a skyline/column-heights drop (a straight drop from above the whole stack can never enter a cavity under an overhang).

(3) "20G" mode (defined p.2-3): pieces move maximally downward INSTANTLY and on their own; the player cannot control descent speed but may still rotate/translate left-right BEFORE the piece locks — i.e., the piece slides along the terrain surface at its landing height, re-dropping after each move. This is STRICTLY MORE permissive at the bottom than hard-drop (surface sliding can tuck under overhangs); the paper's 20G claims are that its specific gadgets remain playable and forced even there.

(4) Movement-insensitive remark: they note most PRIOR hardness results work under "most reasonable rotation models" [BDH+04 Sec 6.4]; by contrast, most of THIS paper's Section 4/6 results specifically REQUIRE SRS kicks.

Objectives: "Tetris clearing" = place all given pieces and end with an empty board; "Tetris survival" = place all given pieces without ever losing. Board dimensions are generalized (width Theta(n), height Theta(t poly(n)) with the 3-Partition integers in unary — strongly NP-complete source, so board area is polynomial). No bag randomizer anywhere: sequences are arbitrary; 7-bag-legal hardness is explicitly posed as an OPEN PROBLEM (p.23).

## theorems

MAIN RESULTS (all offline, perfect information, prefilled initial board, generalized dimensions, reductions from 3-Partition with Distinct Integers / Numerical 3DM with Distinct Integers, which Theorem 3.3 proves strongly ASP-complete):

- Theorem 4.11 (via Props 4.1-4.9): For ANY size-2 subset A of the 7 tetromino types, Tetris CLEARING with SRS is NP-hard and the counting problem is #P-hard, even when the sequence contains only pieces in A. Breakdown: Prop 4.1 = {I,X} for all X (adapts Total Tetris bottles); Prop 4.2 = {O,J},{O,L}; Prop 4.3 = {O,S},{O,Z}; Prop 4.4 = {O,T}; Prop 4.5 = {S,T},{Z,T}; Prop 4.6 = {S,Z}; Prop 4.7 = {J,Z},{J,T},{L,S},{L,T}; Prop 4.8 = {J,S},{L,Z}; Prop 4.9 = {J,L}.
- Prop 4.1 additionally holds in HARD DROPS ONLY mode for {I,J}, {I,L}, {I,O}, {I,T} (marked H in Table 1), and in 20G mode for {I,J}, {I,L} (marked G). NOT for {I,S},{I,Z} in those modes (they need an S/Z spin, Fig 27). Conjecture 4.10: the {J,L} reduction (Prop 4.9) works under 20G (the "G?" in Table 1) — unproven.
- Theorem 5.1: Tetris SURVIVAL is NP-COMPLETE in the "hard drops only" AND "20G" game modes, even with pieces restricted to {I, O}. Sequence structure: per 3-Partition element a_i, the run (O^{n/3+1}, I^{a_i}, O); initial board = n/3 width-1 buckets of height 4t separated by width-1 walls, one blocked bucket of height t-1, one extra column, two empty top rows. Improves Temprano [Tem15] (hard-drops-only hardness with FIVE piece types {L,J,I,Z,T}).
- Corollary 5.2: Tetris CLEARING is NP-hard under "hard drops only" or "20G" with piece set {I, O, T} (extends Thm 5.1's instance with finale (T, I^t, O^{2n/3}, T^3)). Remark: this shows a single configuration where surviving and clearing are simultaneously hard in these modes; also their only 20G clearing result not subsumed by 2-piece results.
- Theorem 6.1: Tetris clearing with SRS is ASP-COMPLETE for piece sets {I,T,L} and {I,T,J} (parsimonious reduction from Numerical 3DM with Distinct Integers; "priming buckets" paradigm; implies NP-hardness of finding another solution given k solutions, and #P-completeness). Requires I-spins (Fig 19) — SRS-only.
- Theorem 3.3 (independent interest): 3-Partition with Distinct Integers and Numerical 3DM with Distinct Integers are strongly ASP-complete (chain: 3SAT -> Positive 1-in-3SAT -> Tripartite Edge-Disjoint Triangle Partition -> Numerical 3DM distinct -> 3-Partition distinct).
- Open problems (p.23): complexity with ONE piece type; ASP-completeness for other 3-piece/2-piece sets; survival for 2-piece sets in the STANDARD model; hold pieces; and hardness under the 7-BAG randomizer (explicitly open).

Sequence structure everywhere: long runs of repeated single piece types, S_1...S_n then finale F, each S_i = priming + filling (Theta(n a_i) pieces) + closing. Nowhere near bag-legal. Initial board: always a large polynomial-height prefilled garbage structure ("bottles"/"buckets" + optional "finisher"). Objectives are per-sequence (finite horizon); nothing about infinite play, online adversaries, or empty boards.

## gadgets

SECTION 4 BOTTLE PARADIGM (clearing, SRS): initial board has n/3 tall "bottles" (neck portion with n constant-size "top segments" + body portion with t poly(n)-size "units"), plus a "finisher" (J/L/T structure, Fig 4) whose sole role is to prevent body rows from clearing before all top segments clear; there is exactly one way to clear each finisher type. Per a_i: PRIMING sequence blocks all bottles but one at the same top segment (misuse => overflow or "clog"); FILLING sequence of Theta(a_i) pieces fills a_i units in the open bottle's body (unit size > neck size, so overfill => overflow); CLOSING sequence clears that top-segment row and resets necks. Finale F clears finishers/leftovers. Forcing = pure capacity/parity accounting (general argument, Sec 4.1) + exhaustive "clog" case analysis (Appendix B) showing every improper placement makes some dotted square unfillable without overflow.

TRAJECTORY NEEDS PER SETUP: {I,J}/{I,L}/{I,O}/{I,T} bottles (Figs 5-6): top segments are open-from-above notches; blocking piece straight-drops into the notch; filling I pieces straight-drop down the 2-wide shaft; NO kicks, NO last-second rotations — authors state all pieces can be hard-dropped from the top (these are the H-mode entries). {I,S}/{I,Z}: same shape but the S/Z must SPIN (kick, Fig 27) into the uppermost top segment — kick-dependent. ALL other pairs (Props 4.2-4.9) are intrinsically kick/tuck-dependent: pieces must be smuggled THROUGH a covered neck under overhangs into the body via multi-step SRS spin sequences — J spin (Fig 20), J spins for {J,S} (Figs 21-23), L-version spins (Figs 23-24), the {O,J} setup needs a 5-step J-spin staircase sequence (Fig 25), S spin chains (Figs 26-29), T-spin triple-step (Fig 30). These placements are inside cavities unreachable by any straight drop.

THEOREM 5.1 BUCKET GADGET (survival, {I,O}, hard-drop/20G — THE key gadget for us): board = n/3 width-1 buckets, height 4t, separated by width-1 full-height walls; leftmost extra structure = a bucket of height t-1 blocked by one square with an open diagonal square (inaccessible until Cor 5.2's finale); one extra column for even width; TWO EMPTY ROWS on top. Per a_i the run (O^{n/3+1}, I^{a_i}, O): (a) PRIMING O^{n/3+1}: each O straight-drops onto the tops of two adjacent walls, roofing one bucket mouth; parity forces the player to roof all but one bucket, leaving either one 2x2 gap or two 1x1-wide gaps; 1-wide gaps create forever-unfillable cells => eventual overflow, so a rational player leaves the 2x2 gap. (b) FILLING I^{a_i}: each I is rotated vertical AT THE TOP and straight-dropped down the single open width-1 bucket (only vertical I fits width 1; buckets have height ≡ 0 mod 4 so no I ever sticks out partially); if the player deviates from a correct partition, at some point an extra I arrives with no open bucket and cannot fit the 2x2 gap (length 4) => loss. (c) CLOSING O: only fits the 2x2 gap; completes and CLEARS the top two rows, resetting the roof for the next round. Survival of the whole sequence <=> the a_i were routed as an exact 3-partition (every bucket filled to exactly 4t at the end, Fig 16e). Trajectories: EVERY placement is a straight drop ("no overhangs in any of the buckets"); no kick, no tuck, no climb, no mid-fall move; O never rotates (can't under SRS anyway); I rotates only in the open top rows. Under 20G the same placements are reachable by surface-sliding (O can't fall into width-1 buckets so it slides on top; I slides over placed Os to the open bucket).

COROLLARY 5.2 FINALE ({I,O,T} clearing): append (T, I^t, O^{2n/3}, T^3). First T straight-drops onto the diagonal notch of the blocked left bucket and clears a row, opening it; I^t fills that bucket clearing everything but the last two rows; the last I protrudes one square (into the empty top rows) because of the earlier clear; O^{2n/3} fills the 2n/3 x 4 space on the right; three T pieces fill the remainder and fully clear the board. All placements again straight drops ("All of these pieces can be placed in both special game modes").

ASP GADGET (Thm 6.1, SRS only): "priming buckets" — I piece is the primer and must be SPUN (Fig 19 kick) into a horizontal slot inside the bucket; T and L placements then forced one-by-one; finale (L, I^N) clears rightmost columns. Kick-dependent, does not concern hard-drop mode.

## compat

ASSESSMENT FOR OUR MODEL (skyline hard drop, rotation+column chosen before the drop, no kicks/tucks/slides, lock on skyline contact, loss iff height > m AFTER clears):

KEY EQUIVALENCE: The paper's "hard drops only" mode = our action model whenever the spawn/top rows are open. Pre-drop free rotation+translation at the top = choosing (piece, rotation, column); a hard drop from above the entire stack under cell-collision is IDENTICAL to a skyline drop (a piece falling straight from above rests on the topmost filled cell of each footprint column — it can never enter a cavity, same as our engine); SRS kicks are inert because kicks fire only when the in-place rotation is obstructed, and rotations happen in empty top rows. In every H-mode construction the authors explicitly keep the top rows empty and state no kicks/last-second rotations are used. Therefore both directions of each H-mode reduction (the intended strategy AND the soundness case analysis, which quantifies over exactly the set of hard-drop placements) apply verbatim to our engine.

TRANSFERS VERBATIM:
1. Theorem 5.1 => our P1 (OFFLINE-HD survival) is NP-COMPLETE, even with the sequence restricted to {I,O}, on a prefilled board of width ~(2/3)n+O(1) and height 4t+2, generalized (n,m). NP membership is trivial in our model (guess placements, simulate). Loss-rule check: the gadget's forcing is via unfillable space causing genuine overflow (extra I of length 4 can't fit a 2x2 gap; vertical placement exceeds board top with no clear) — our "loss iff height>m after clears" reproduces every forced loss; our clears-before-loss-check rule if anything matches BETTER (Cor 5.2's first T transiently pokes then clears). Minor formal caveat: their loss also triggers on spawn blocking; ours doesn't — but no branch of their argument relies on spawn blocking (all losses are overflow losses), so the equivalence of YES/NO instances is preserved.
2. Corollary 5.2 => our P1 clearing variant ("place all pieces and end with empty board") is NP-hard with {I,O,T}.
3. Prop 4.1, H-marked setups => our P1 clearing variant is NP-hard AND #P-hard (counting by final placements) for piece alphabets {I,J}, {I,L}, {I,O}, {I,T}. (#P transfers because in these setups the reachable-placement sets under SRS-hard-drop and under our model coincide.)
Net for our model at generalized dims: P1 survival NP-complete ({I,O}); P1 clearing NP-hard/#P-hard ({I,O},{I,J},{I,L},{I,T}, and {I,O,T} from the same board that is survival-hard).

BREAKS / DOES NOT TRANSFER:
- Prop 4.1 for {I,S},{I,Z} and ALL of Props 4.2-4.9 (every 2-subset of {O,T,S,Z,J,L}) and Theorem 4.11 as a universal 2-piece statement: these REQUIRE SRS spins/kicks to move pieces through covered necks under overhangs (Appendix A, Figs 19-30). In our model a piece can never pass under an overhang, so the bottle necks are simply unreachable; the constructions collapse. Hardness for those pairs in our model is OPEN (not disproven — just unproven; new overhang-free gadgets would be needed).
- Theorem 6.1 ASP-completeness: kick-dependent (I-spin primer). Breaks.
- 20G results: a DIFFERENT model from ours (20G permits post-drop surface sliding, i.e., tucking under overhangs — strictly more placements than straight drops). Their 20G claims are extra robustness of the same instances, not statements about our model; ignore except as reassurance that the {I,O} gadget is very forcing.
- P1' (bag-legal offline): NOTHING transfers. Every reduction uses runs of Theta(n a_i) identical pieces from a 2-3 letter alphabet — maximally non-bag. The paper explicitly poses 7-bag hardness as an open problem (p.23). Under bag legality every 7-window contains all 7 types, destroying every withholding-based forcing gadget. P1' is unclassified by this paper.
- P2 (online adversarial horizon), P3 (Atlas membership / survive forever), P4 (empty-board dims): NOT ADDRESSED anywhere. The paper is purely offline/finite-sequence, always with a large prefilled initial board. It contributes no upper or lower bound on the game-theoretic (AND-OR) or infinite-horizon problems, and nothing about empty-board starts. (For canonical fixed 10x20 all these problems are finite-state anyway; the paper's hardness is only about scaling dimensions.)

STRATEGIC TAKEAWAYS FOR THE ATLAS PROJECT: (a) our no-kick skyline model is exactly the fragment of Tetris where this paper's kick machinery is unavailable — only the I-carried gadgets survive, confirming that OUR model's offline survival is already NP-complete with just {I,O} and garbage; (b) hardness needs three things our Atlas setting removes: adversarially chosen non-bag sequences, polynomial-height prefilled garbage, and generalized dimensions — so no obstruction to the Atlas program follows; (c) the {I,O} bucket gadget is a reusable forcing pattern (parity roofing + width-1 shafts + mod-4 heights) expressible natively in our 162-action engine, useful for building hard test instances or lower-bound experiments for our own P1 solver.
