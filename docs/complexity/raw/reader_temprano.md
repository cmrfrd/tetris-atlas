## model

TEMPRANO'S MODEL (Section 1, p1): the Brzustowski variant. The piece may be translated and rotated freely ("all the time we want") in the FIRST (top) row; it does not fall until the player commits it downward; once committed it falls straight down with no further movement or rotation, and locks where it lands. So the effective action space is exactly (piece, rotation, column) chosen at the top, followed by a gravity-only straight drop onto the skyline. No slides, no tucks, no spins, no soft-drop steering, no kicks (rotation happens over empty top rows, so kick tables never come into play). Pieces can NEVER enter a cavity under an overhang. Lock is immediate on contact. Line clears: standard full-row clears; crucially the paper states explicitly (p8, Lemma 3 proof) that after a clear, blocks do NOT cascade into gaps — rows shift down rigidly ("it will not fall otherwise, even if there are gaps that are not filled under it"), matching the tetris-atlas engine's naive clear. Loss rule: standard "game ends when blocks reach the top" (p2), but loss never plays a role in the reduction (intended play stays 5 rows below the ceiling, and any deviation is already fatal for the CLEARING objective by counting). Objective: clear the board completely. Information model: fully OFFLINE — the entire piece sequence is part of the input (more information than our 1-piece-visibility online game). No hold, no bag.

COMPARISON TO OUR ENGINE (skyline hard drop, no kicks): effectively IDENTICAL for this construction. Two nuances: (a) In Brzustowski's model lateral traversal happens physically in the top row, so a stack reaching the top row could block access to some columns; Temprano neutralizes this by adding 5 spare rows above all structure (board height 16+4T+5 vs walls of height 16+4T, p2: "five extra lines to decide the movements of the pieces"). In our model actions teleport to any column, so the spare rows are unnecessary — our model's placement set is a superset in general, and EQUAL on his boards. Since all his soundness lemmas enumerate ALL (rotation, column) placements — exactly our action space — both directions of the reduction transfer verbatim. (b) His loss check is "blocks reach the top" vs our "height > m after clears"; irrelevant here because no line can clear until the final I-phase (the 1-wide fill column stays empty until then, so no row can complete) and intended play never exceeds the wall height.

## theorems

Theorem 1 (p2, quoted from Garey–Johnson): 3-PARTITION is NP-complete in the strong sense (multiset A of 3s positive numbers with B/4 < a_i < B/2, sum = sB; partition into s triples each summing to B — the paper writes T for B).

Theorem 2 (p13): "Clearing the board in this Tetris variant is NP-hard." (The introduction claims NP-completeness and calls it a 13-years-open problem from Demaine–Hohenberger–Liben-Nowell; NP membership is never argued in the paper, though it is easy: 3-PARTITION is strongly NP-complete so numbers may be unary, the board is (4s+3) x (16+4B+5), the sequence has 3s + 3sB + 3s + s + (4+B) pieces — all polynomial — and a placement sequence is a poly-size poly-checkable certificate.)

PROBLEM SHAPE: input = generalized board dimensions + a specific initial board (pre-filled garbage) + an explicit, fully-known piece sequence; question = can the player (offline, one placement per piece, Brzustowski/skyline movement) clear the entire board. This is the "clear the board" variant of P1 OFFLINE-HD. Board: width 4s+3, height 16+4B+5, with s+2 full-height-(16+4B) wall columns. Piece sequence (structured, instance-derived, NOT free): for each number a_i in given order: [LG] + (T,T,RG) repeated a_i times + [RG, LS]; then s LGs; then 4+B I-pieces. Uses only 5 of the 7 tetromino types (left gun=J, right gun=L, T, left snake=S, I); the square (O) and right snake (Z) never appear.

## gadgets

1. BUCKET (pp2-3, Fig 1): s independent 3-wide wells, each with a full-height (16+4T) separator wall on its left; two more walls flank a 1-wide FILL COLUMN at the far right. Total width 4s+3. Because walls are FULL height, any piece whose footprint touches a wall column rests ON TOP of the wall (row 16+4T+1) — in the skyline model buckets are perfectly isolated 3-wide sub-games. Each bucket starts with 8 cells of planted garbage (a right gun with a left snake on top) forming the "closed" surface.

2. OPEN gadget (Fig 2, Lemma 10 p12): one left gun (J), dropped in the unique gap-free (rotation, column) among the 6 that fit a 3-wide bucket, converts the closed surface into the "open" surface. Which bucket to open is the ONLY free choice — it encodes which subset the upcoming number joins.

3. DIGIT gadget (Figs 3-4, Lemma 8 pp10-11): each unit of a number = two T pieces + one right gun = 12 cells = exactly 4 rows of the 3-wide bucket, placed in a unique interlocking way that reproduces the same notched surface (self-similar, so digits stack). A number a_i = a_i digit units = 4·a_i rows. All placements are straight drops onto the notched skyline; the interlock works precisely BECAUSE no tucks exist — the enumerations of all 6 (T) / 6 (T) / 12 (RG) drop placements show every alternative creates a permanent cavity or a 1-wide slot fillable only by I.

4. CLOSE gadget (Fig 5, Lemma 9 pp11-12): right gun + left snake, unique gap-free placement pair, returns the bucket to the closed surface (now 4(a_i+1) rows taller). Open+close overhead = 12 cells = one extra "phantom unit" per number — this is what makes the subset-size arithmetic work.

5. TOP-NOTCH cleanup (p8): after all 3s numbers, each bucket top has a notch; s left guns fill them (one per bucket; a second LG on a flush bucket would sit above the line — fatal).

6. I-DRAIN (p8, Lemmas 1-2): 4+T vertical I pieces dropped into the 1-wide fill column, exactly filling its 16+4T rows; as each I lands, 4 full rows complete and clear bottom-up. No other piece can put a single cell into the 1-wide column (everything else is ≥2 wide in every rotation, so it lands on the wall tops — fatal by Lemma 3).

FORCING MECHANISM (Lemmas 1-10, pp8-13): pure straight-drop skyline forcing — no tucks, kicks, or climbs anywhere, in either direction of the reduction. Three layers: (i) EXACT SUPPLY: total piece material = exactly the empty space below the 16+4T line; I re-verified: per bucket, supply 3·LG(12) + digits(12T) + 3·(RG+LS)(24) + final LG(4) = 12T+40 = 3(16+4T) − 8 garbage; fill 4(4+T) = 16+4T; globally, walls+garbage+supply = (16+4T)(4s+3) = exactly 16+4T full lines. So clearing the board ⇔ zero-waste perfect packing; any permanently-empty cell or any material above the line is fatal (Lemmas 3-4). (ii) CAVITY PERMANENCE: in this model a cavity can never be refilled, and no clear can remove it (its row can never complete; no clears happen at all before the I-phase since the fill column gates every row). (iii) FINITE ENUMERATION: Lemmas 5-7 (RG: 6 ways, T: 6 ways, LS: 3 ways into a closed bucket — all fatal), Lemma 8 (digit pieces: unique gap-free chain), Lemma 9 (close: 3 gap-free RG placements narrowed to 1 by the LS), Lemma 10 (open: unique). Subset-size cheating is killed by arithmetic: a bucket holding k numbers summing to sigma is exactly full iff 8 + 12·sigma + 12k + 4 = 48+12T, i.e. sigma + k = T+3; with B/4 < a_i < B/2, k=2 forces sigma < T (impossible) and k=4 forces sigma > T (impossible), so k=3 and sigma=T — a faithful 3-partition.

## compat

TRANSFER TO OUR SKYLINE-HARD-DROP NO-KICK MODEL:

SURVIVES VERBATIM — P1-CLEAR: The movement model is functionally identical to ours: his lemmas enumerate placements as (rotation, column) straight drops onto the skyline with no cavity entry, which IS our 162-action-style space; his walls-plus-5-headroom design makes Brzustowski top-row traversal equal to our teleport-to-column semantics on every reachable position of the construction (and our model being weakly more permissive in general doesn't help the player: the enumerations already cover our full action set). Lock rule identical; clear rule identical (rigid row shift, no cascade — he states this explicitly on p8, matching our engine); loss-rule difference (post-clear check) never engaged. Therefore: OFFLINE-HD with the CLEAR-the-board objective, arbitrary initial board, explicit sequence, generalized (n,m), is NP-hard in our exact model, and NP-complete with the (easy, unstated) membership argument. Note it needs n to grow (width 4s+3): says nothing about fixed n=10.

DOES NOT FOLLOW — P1-SURVIVE: his instances are trivially survivable (total material ≈ 4T+14 rows per 3-wide bucket vs 16+4T+5 height; just dump pieces). Survival-hardness in the skyline model would need an adaptation (e.g., tighten m and append a junk flood that tops out iff the board wasn't cleared) — plausible but genuinely new work, not in the paper.

DOES NOT FOLLOW — P1' OFFLINE-HD-BAG: the sequence is grossly bag-illegal: runs of (T,T,RG) repeated a_i times, then 4+B CONSECUTIVE I pieces (7-bag allows at most 2 adjacent I's, at bag boundaries), and two piece types (O, Z) never appear at all, while any bag-legal prefix delivers every type at rate 1/7. Bag-legalizing would require gadgets that absorb a forced O and Z every 7 pieces — an open redesign problem; O in a 3-wide bucket is particularly hostile to his surfaces.

DOES NOT FOLLOW — P2/P3/P4: P2's adversary is bag-constrained and cannot emit his sequence, and a one-player puzzle lower bound doesn't transfer to the AND-OR game value anyway; P3 (gfp/Atlas membership, infinite horizon) and P4 (empty-board dims-only) are untouched — nothing in the paper concerns infinite play, adaptivity, or empty-board reachability (he cites Hoogeboom–Kosters [6] for board constructibility, but that's for the standard slide-enabled model).

CORRECTNESS ASSESSMENT (non-peer-reviewed preprint; no published rebuttal or erratum found; cited approvingly by later work incl. FUN 2024 "Tetris with Few Piece Types", arxiv.org/abs/2404.10712): ARCHITECTURE SOUND, DETAILS FIGURE-BASED. Strong points I independently verified: (1) the global cell count is EXACT — walls + garbage + all supplied pieces = (16+4T)(4s+3) cells = exactly 16+4T full lines, so clearing ⇔ zero-waste packing and every "wasted cell ⇒ dead" appeal is airtight; (2) per-bucket and fill-column counts balance exactly (12T+40 and 16+4T); (3) the sigma+k = T+3 bucket-fullness equation plus B/4<a_i<B/2 provably blocks 2-number/4-number subset cheats; (4) the design eliminates the two classic soundness leak classes: no clears are possible before the I-phase (fill column gates every row), and cavities are permanent in a no-tuck model, so any deviation is immediately and provably fatal rather than "probably" fatal; (5) full-height walls make buckets independent, so the case analysis is genuinely local to a 3-wide well; (6) enumeration cardinalities are consistent with the action space (6 placements for 4-rotation pieces in a 3-wide well, 3 for 2-rotation snakes, 12 for RG across the two mid-digit surfaces). Weak points: (a) Lemmas 5-10 are proved by raster figures asserted to enumerate "all ways" — I could not pixel-verify every panel, and completeness of the reachable-gap-free-surface induction is implicit rather than stated (the invariant "at every step the only gap-free-reachable states are: all-closed, or exactly-one-open with an intended digit surface" is used but never formalized); (b) the s-left-guns top-notch step has no dedicated lemma; (c) Theorem 2 says NP-hard while the intro claims NP-complete, and membership is unargued; (d) prose quality is poor (typos, informal statements), typical of a solo unrefereed preprint. VERDICT: high confidence (~90%) the theorem and reduction are correct for our model; the residual risk is confined to the finite case-bash panels, which are small enough that our own engine could machine-verify every lemma (enumerate all placements on each of the ~6 gadget surfaces) and even model-check whole small instances end-to-end — a cheap and worthwhile validation if we want to cite this as the P1-CLEAR hardness anchor for the Atlas complexity map. Sources: arxiv.org/abs/1506.07204 (the paper), arxiv.org/pdf/2404.10712 and arxiv.org/abs/2009.14336 and arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0210020 (context: later/standard-model hardness lines).
